## A long PDF table Segment times out after Fish Audio starts returning audio (2026-11-21 01:35, issue #262)

In Zotero 10.0.9, a PDF had 456 Segments at sentence granularity, with
a median length of 70 characters. The body of Table 2 occupied one
3,231-character Segment (3,235 UTF-8 bytes) and its continuation
occupied another of 2,701 characters. The first had no line breaks.
In the installed `resource/reader/reader.js`, `segmentChain` splits
sentences at lines 71660–71677. Its size fallback accepts text up to
5,000 UTF-8 bytes as one piece (30741, 71092–71095).

With the Player paused, the installed OpenReader 1.17.2-beta2 reader
interface was asked for uncached audio on the selected Fish Audio voice
and model (`s2.1-pro-free`). An observer timed the first response event
carrying audio. Each row is one live request. Ready means the interface
returned a complete clip, before decoding or playback.

| Input | Characters sent | First audio | Ready or error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Ordinary sentence | 155 | 4.47 s | Ready at 4.69 s |
| Table 2 Segment | 3,229 after text cleaning | 17.09 s | Network error at 60.08 s |
| First 286 characters of the same table | 286 | 14.82 s | Ready at 14.99 s |

All three requests received HTTP 200. The table request returned zero
playable bytes to the Engine. The error console recorded
`fish: no audio within 60 s`. The short table extract returned 35.16 s
of audio and decoded in 0.11 s. This probe did not play the document or
measure end-to-end speaker latency.

Fish Audio's `speak` reads the entire event stream with
`await response.text()` before it assembles the audio and aligns the
words (`src/core/providers/fish.ts`). The reader interface gives the
whole synthesis 60 s, including this read, and aborts it at expiry
(`src/read-aloud/remote-interface.ts`). An offline replay through the
Fish provider, reader interface and Engine delivered audio immediately
and held the stream open for ten virtual seconds. The Engine started
only after the stream closed. The same effect reproduced with an
eight-character input and two packets. A short single-packet response
started immediately.

## Each table piece has a native source range before playback (2026-11-21 01:52, issue #262)

Zotero 10.0.9's `_requestReadAloudSegments` materializes positions on
the Segment array before looking up the selection or saved start and
calling `setSegments` (`resource/reader/reader.js` 84134–84143).
At that boundary OpenReader can still divide an oversized Segment.
The native collection's `addSegment` normalizes a raw range from its
SDT chain and registers the new Segment in its source map (71298–71318).
Each piece can then use the native word-span and annotation lookup, and
the manager sees the same array as the start lookup. Only the first
piece inherits the original paragraph anchor.

Replaying the captured 3,231 characters through that installed SDT
collection, the reader interface and the Engine produced 15 pieces,
at most 239 characters each. Every native word span over a full piece
resolved to that piece's original text. In the controlled replay the
first 159-character request started playback while later requests were
pending. The same replay with subdivision disabled requested 3,229
characters and waited for the delayed tail.

In the installed 1.17.2-beta3 package, a disposable PDF made from the
same text had a separately recognized caption and 14 table pieces of
up to 239 characters. Playback reached the first 173-character piece
8.27 seconds after Play. Two later requests were held, the audio clock
advanced and the first piece had both sentence and word source spans.
The probe did not independently count bytes from a fresh Fish request.
A separate attempt to inject a failure at a later piece did not induce
the intended Engine error. Failed-piece Retry and paragraph Skip are
covered by the deterministic playback regression.
