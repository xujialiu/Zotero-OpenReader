## A pending switch let the old voice ask for sentences the new voice would read, and a jump called it off (2026-10-01 01:53, issue #163)

The owner asked that a voice switch stop the old voice's requests. Read
from the code at `d4b9781` (1.16.2), four ways the old voice kept asking
while a switch was pending:

- The Engine's read-ahead went on for the old voice, and so did the
  plugin's prefetch chain (`remote-interface.ts` `prefetchAfter`), which
  runs one request at a time through its whole list of sentences ahead
  once it has started, checking only that the reader is alive. A server
  that answers one request at a time worked through all of them before
  the new voice's first sentence.
- At a sentence the old voice had no audio for, it requested it: the
  Handoff took over only at a word of the sentence playing or at a later
  sentence whose new-voice audio it already held (`sentenceStart`).
- After #154's skip commit the old store was closed but not aborted: its
  read-ahead requests on their way finished, and the prefetch chain went
  on for the old voice.
- voice-pick shadowed `jumpTo` and `repositionTo` with a cancel, and the
  session's `start` on a jump cancelled too: a jump read the new place in
  the old voice and dropped what the new voice had prepared.

Now, while a switch is pending, the session's old voice reads only
through `ClipStore.held`: the decoded clip, a fetch of its own on its way,
or what the interface answers to `getAudio(…, { held: true })` — the
plugin's cache, or a synthesis of the same key already in `pending` (the
prefetch's included). That lookup never reaches a provider, is counted as
`lookups`, not `requests`, and answers `not-held` for a Zotero voice,
whose audio the plugin does not keep. Nothing is read ahead while a
switch is pending; a read-ahead already running stops before its next
request. The prefetch chain asks `mayPrefetch(voiceId)` at its start and
before each request; the Engine answers no while the tab's session has a
switch pending or reads another voice.

No held clip means the reading waits for the new voice there
(`Handoff.waitAt`): the poll loads that sentence before anything ahead,
and its arrival is the ordinary sentence commit, so the manager is told of
the new voice only once that voice's audio is in hand. A failure or a
cancel while the reading waits reaches the session (`handoffEnded`), and
one task later the old voice asks for the sentence: one task, because
voice-pick's cancel on `deactivate` or `clearSegments` runs before the
manager destroys its controller, and the session ends only in the
Engine's microtask after that; an immediate request would have gone out
for a reading that was ending.

A skip no longer cancels or commits: it tells the Handoff it `moved()`,
which restarts the preparation at the sentence landed on, and the
session's sentence start applies the rule there — the new voice if it has
the audio, else the old voice if it holds it, else the wait.
`sentenceStart` refused a sentence equal to `currentIndex`, which is how a
resume of the old voice's paused clip is told from a new start; a skip
that lands on the sentence last started (a skip back from the first
sentence) sets `landing`, so the new voice can still take it. A jump over
the same voice and segments while a switch is pending resets the run in
place (`EngineSession.reset`) instead of `start`, keeping the old store
and the Handoff: Zotero's `repositionTo` (10.0.3, reader.js 82621-82628)
keeps `_segments` and moves only `backwardStopIndex`, so the prepared
clips' indices stay valid. `Handoff.commitNow`, `EngineSession.adoptVoice`
and the `skip` boundary kind of #154 are gone; `voiceSwitch().mechanism`
is `engine-handoff-v2`, and its report gains `waitedAt` and `oldRequests`.

Not touched: #162, the new voice's store keeping the preparation's signal
after a takeover, which switches the prefetch off for it.

## Verified live, and the start from a selection still called the switch off (2026-10-01 04:38, issue #163)

`zotero-tester` verified 1.16.3-beta6 (xpi `564d6e82…`, commit
`05b4a15`) on macOS with the configured Kokoro server's `af_bella` /
`af_alloy`, the new voice's requests held by a wrapper on the sandbox's
`fetch`: every row of case 4a item 14 passed — the wait at the first
sentence the old voice lacked (`waitedAt` 16 and 17, `store.requests`
flat, zero old-voice fetches after the pick), skips past the read-ahead
by `Shift+→` and the player's button, `←` to a sentence the old voice
had (a word cut 5.16 s into it), failure and speed while waiting, paused
reuse and the paused skip, and the prefetch's `stopped, the voice is
switching`. The run took two sessions: the first spent its 30 minutes
seeding the fixture.

Two things it measured. `oldRequests` is written at `finish()`, so on a
failure or a cancel it stays 0 and the old voice's one request after it
shows only as `store.requests` +1. And the plugin's audio cache, keyed by
text for the life of the process, answers `held` lookups for every
sentence either voice has read, so each row that needs a sentence the old
voice lacks needs fresh text.

Its jump row went through `manager.repositionTo`, and the tester found
that the user's own start from a selection does not: with a session open
`Shift+Space` is Play/Pause (`smartPlay`), and on the unpause Zotero
restarts from the selection itself — `_onReadAloudEngineStateChanged`
(reader.js 10.0.3, 83880-83885) calls `manager.clearSegments()`, and
`_requestReadAloudSegments` (84048-84073) rebuilds the same sentences as
new objects and calls `setSegments(segments, selectionIndex, null)` after
`await this._loadSDT()`. voice-pick cancelled on `clearSegments`, and the
teardown ended the session in the Engine's microtask before the rebuild
bound, which cancelled again.

Now `clearSegments` leaves voice-pick's list, and an end the manager's
teardown causes parks a pending switch with the old voice's store
(`EngineSession.end({ park: true })`, the Engine's `destroyed`). The next
`bind` of the same voice over the same texts — the rebuild, or new
segments bound while the session is live — takes it back as a jump,
rebasing the store and the Handoff onto the new array (indices and texts
equal); other segments cancel it and close the store. The window holds
because everything from the unpause to the rebuild's `setSegments` runs in
microtasks — `_loadSDT` returns its cached structure, and the
skipped-lines shadow of it (#87) only chains promises — while the
Handoff's poll, which would find a parked switch invalid and call it off,
is a 25 ms timer. Decoded clips survive the output's close in between:
the device-change rebuild already replays a clip in a new AudioContext.

## The Top bar lay over the Appearance popup's first row, and the player now sits under every Zotero popup (2026-10-01 14:35, issue #164)

**What the owner saw.** With the player open as the Top bar, the
default, an EPUB's Appearance popup (the toolbar's `#appearance`,
reader.js:30253) seemed to start at Columns. Its Page Layout row
(Paginated / Scrolled, reader.js:36655–36676) was gone, and the owner
took scrolled mode to have been dropped by Zotero. A first look from a
screenshot blamed Zotero. It was the player: the top band of the
screenshot was the Top bar, its layout button at the right end.

**The geometry.** Zotero hangs the popup at `top: 38px`, with
`padding: 12px` and `gap: 12px` (reader.css:525, 527), and rows 22 px
high (`.split-toggle`, reader.css:567). Its first row is therefore at
50–72. The Top bar's frame was `position: fixed; z-index: 10000` at
41–75, over the popup's overlay (`.toolbar-popup-overlay`, `z-index: 50`,
reader.css:382). Measured on 10.0.5-beta.2 with the return-key EPUB and a
1411 × 887 window: `elementFromPoint` at the centers of Paginated and
Scrolled answered `iframe#ztts-player-frame`, three runs out of three,
while Columns (84–106) answered itself. On a PDF or a snapshot, the row
under the bar is Reading mode (reader.js:36699–36703). The toolbar's own
Reading mode button (30088) still reaches that one.

**No ancestor caps Zotero's popup layers.** `#reader-ui`, its
`.split-view`, `.primary-view` and `.secondary-view` carry no z-index
(reader.css:1112–1114). So the overlays (50), `.find-popup` and
`.view-popup` (70), `.context-menu-overlay` and `.custom-select-overlay`
(100), `.error-bar` (90) and `body.freeze:before` (80) all stack in the
root context with the frame. Raising `.toolbar-popup-overlay` and
`.find-popup` to 10001 put both over the frame. The raise was measured
and turned down: the popup's **+** opens the new-theme dialog with
Appearance still open (`onAddTheme` sets only `themePopup`,
reader.js:83221), and that dialog's `.dialog-popup-overlay` stays at 50.
Its name field and Cancel answered the raised overlay.

**Now.** While the Top bar shows, `.appearance-popup` gets
`margin-top: 34px`, the same rule the find bar has had since #135; its
top lands at 72, 3 px over the bar's edge, as Zotero lays it over the
toolbar. The frame's z-index is 35 (`FRAME_Z`, `src/ui/player-cover.ts`),
between the toolbar (30, reader.css:1000) and Zotero's lowest popup layer
(`.utility-popup`, 40). It is above the sidebar (10) and its resizers
(20, 21), which a dragged Floating panel can reach; by default the
panel sits at the document area's left + 10. Measured with the frame at 35: Page Layout at 50–72, the find
box at 56 with its margin removed, and the theme dialog all answered
themselves.

**What 35 also changes.** Every popup Zotero draws lies over the player
now: the selection and annotation popups and the link preview, which
#135 had accepted lying partly under a bar; the context menus; the error
bar. Zotero's freeze tint (`body.freeze`, reader.js:83518–83523) now
covers the player like the rest of the reader. Before, the player stayed
clickable through it. While one of the player's own menus is open, its
frame grows (#135), and a Zotero popup that meets the menu is drawn over
it.

**For a later probe.** In a bridge call, `api.wait` in the same call as
`start` came back without `results` several times, and the call
answered a bare `undefined` (the logged `w.results is undefined` lines
at 14:25–14:30 are that, not the plugin). Start in one call and read
`.tmp/zotero-dev/<runId>/results/` from disk. Opening the player on a
fixture writes a `documentVoices.user/<key>` record, which cleanup has
to clear.
