## The reading line splits the free space, and a blank message is a missing one (2026-09-29 04:58, issue #155)

- **Geometry.** `followTarget` places a box with its top at
  `above + (VH - h) * line / 100`: the uncovered viewport's free space is
  split above and below it by the line. At 50 this is exactly the old
  `centerOn`, `(top + bottom) / 2 - VH / 2 - above`, so no earlier
  expectation moved. Two readings of "the sentence at 10%" were turned
  down: its middle at the line cuts a tall sentence's first line off near
  0%, and its top at the line makes 50% something other than centering.
  The split never cuts off a sentence that fits, at any value.
- **What it places.** The whole fitting sentence on entry, clipping and
  explicit return, and the real word of an oversized sentence. The
  oversized sentence's head still goes to the top edge plus
  `followMargin`. Positions the shadow cannot measure (a rotated page, a
  position without rects) still fall back to Zotero's own
  `navigateToPosition` with `block: 'center'`: that path has no line.
- **A change of the line re-places the current sentence** as a change of
  style does: the PDF shadow keys its per-view entry on `style@line`, the
  EPUB record keeps the line beside the style. Paginated EPUB flow leaves
  the line out of that comparison, since a line change must not turn a
  page. Both `inspect`s report `line`.
- **A Fluent message may not be empty in this pane.** English puts
  "% from the top" after the field and Chinese "距顶部" before it, so the
  row carries a message for each side. An English `{ "" }` for the side
  before failed `test/l10n.test.ts` — `paneElementBlank`, the same check
  `diagnostics.l10n()` runs on the live pane, reads an element Fluent left
  empty as untranslated. English's side before the field is "at" instead.

## One unanchored PDF bookmark hid section time for the whole book (2026-09-29 05:15, issue #156)

The owner reported no section time in *The Well-Spoken Thesaurus*. The
owner reads its PDF attachment. Probes ran on temporary copies of both
attachments, under the test WebDAV, with the originals untouched. They
walked the outline depth first and applied every `readingSections` check
to each entry without stopping at the first failure. The EPUB had 57
entries, all with refs, and every check passed. The PDF had 55 entries:
44 passed, and 11 had no `ref`, only
`target: { position: { pageIndex, rect } }` with `source: "native"`.

Those 11 are Zotero's unanchored bookmarks. `snapNativeNodes`
(`resource/document-worker/worker.js` 147503 onward) anchors an entry
only by title in the window after the destination, by transfer from the
first detected heading, or by a heading block within 24 points of the
destination point. `projectNativeSkeleton` (147584–147610) writes the rest
as a bare page destination. That is ordinary for a PDF's own bookmarks,
and `readingSections` returned `[]` at the first such entry.

The fix drops only the interval that an unlocated boundary ends. Such a
boundary is a missing ref, a ref without its own segment, or a ref inside
a segment. The next interval starts at the next located entry.
Order contradictions still refuse the whole outline. Replaying the
captured PDF outline over one synthetic segment per block gives 38 located
sections of 55 entries. Engine diagnostics now expose `readingSection`,
because `sectionTitle` appears only in a `ready` snapshot and a copy
paused at once never becomes ready.

Two probe details matter for later runs. A reader's primary EPUB/PDF view
has no `getReadAloudSegments`. `_primarySDTView` is absent, and
`_requestReadAloudSegments` returns early unless the manager is active.
Segments therefore require an activated session. On a fresh copy,
`selectVoice` of a listed System voice activated it. The owner's default
Kokoro voice was not offered because `local.enabled` was false. Opening
the player first started the copy's document voice, a Fish voice, before
the pick took over. Output was muted, but at most a few Fish requests may
have been made.

## Scroll at every line: the manager clears the word at every segment event, and a pref at its default holds no choice (2026-09-29 07:53, issue #157)

- **When a sentence's word is known.** Zotero's manager sets
  `_activeTimestampIndex = null` on both `ActiveSegmentChanging` and
  `ActiveSegmentChange` (reader.js 82679-82690, Zotero
  10.0.3-beta.3+80bc5565e), so the first push of every sentence has no
  active word (`wordTiming` `none`), even from a voice with word timings.
  The Engine emits `ActiveSegmentChange` from `segmentStart`, when the clip
  is playing, so at that push the controller's
  `getTimestampsForSegment(manager._activeSegment)` already answers:
  real timings, the whole-segment stand-in, or null. `segmentTiming`
  (highlight-style.ts) classifies that answer as `wordTiming` does the
  word. Real timings mean wait for the first word; anything else means
  place the sentence now. A `BufferingChange` also calls `_stateChanged`
  (82675-82678), so a clip that arrives after its sentence was entered
  still pushes.
- **What cannot be known.** A skip while paused emits
  `ActiveSegmentChange` before its clip is fetched (session.ts `skipTo`).
  A sentence not read ahead yet then reads as `none` and is placed whole,
  not by its first line; Play then places the first word's line, a
  second scroll.
- **Lines.** A line is the word's first rect, so a hyphenated word counts
  where it starts. Two boxes are one line when they overlap vertically by
  half the shorter one, which keeps a superscript on its line. A line is
  remembered only when it is placed, never per word. Remembering the
  word's union would take a hyphenated word's two lines for one and miss
  the next line. The placed line is in container (PDF) or document
  (EPUB) coordinates, so scrolling leaves it valid and a zoom re-places.
- **The regime is tracked per sentence.** The per-view entry keeps the
  last regime, `line` or `sentence`. A turn into `sentence` counts as
  entering the sentence, and a turn into `line` forgets the placed line.
  So Shift+W re-places at once, through the new `zotero-tts.highlight.word`
  observer beside the style and line observers. `coming` changes nothing.
- **The default moved everyone who "chose" every sentence.** Gecko drops
  a user value equal to the default. Anyone who picked Scroll at every
  sentence while it was the default holds no user value and follows the
  new default, `line`. The owner accepted that rather than a migration.
  Older copies read `line` through `autoScrollMode`, whose fallback was
  `sentence`, so a synced `line` scrolls at every sentence there.

## A XUL row is a flex row, and a ? beside a wrapping note shrinks to its glyph (2026-09-29 09:55, issue #158)

- **The toolkit.** Zotero 10's `chrome/toolkit/content/global/xul.css`
  (the GRE `omni.ja`, not the app's) opens with `* { display: flex }`:
  every XUL element is a flex box, an `hbox` a flex row. Nothing there
  sets `flex-shrink` or `min-width`, so a child keeps the CSS defaults,
  `flex-shrink: 1` and `min-width: auto`, its min-content width.
  `description` is `display: flow-root` (124-126) and wraps; a
  `label[value]` is `inline-flex` with its text as `::before`, never
  wrapping (138-150).
- **What broke.** `label.ztts-help` was sized by `width` and `height`
  alone. Beside the Zotero section's note, which wraps onto two lines,
  the row ran out of width and the ? gave up its width down to the glyph
  and border while its height stayed 1.25em: an upright oval. A ? after a
  field or a one-line label never met it, because its row had room.
- **The fix.** `flex-shrink: 0` on the ?, pinned by
  `test/ui/pane-css.test.ts`. Any fixed-size decoration in a pane row
  needs the same: a `width` in a XUL box is a preference, not a size.

## Two siblings in a centered row are not on one baseline: the links moved into their text's line (2026-09-29 11:38, issue #159)

- **What the owner saw** on 1.16.2-beta6: "Add more time" sat lower than
  "114 credits left". The run had measured it on the Log in pair (link box
  1.5 px below the reason's) and passed it within a 2 px tolerance that
  should have been 0.
- **Why.** The two were siblings in an `hbox align="center"`, which
  centers boxes, not text. The toolkit gives `xul|description` and
  `xul|label` `margin-block: 1px 2px` (`global-shared.css` 264-268), and
  Zotero's `.zotero-text-link { margin: 0 }` (`preferences.css`) takes the
  link's away, so the two boxes centered differently. The margins account
  for 0.5 px of the 1.5 measured; the rest was not traced, since the fix
  does not depend on it.
- **The fix** (e86555c): each link is the next sibling of an `html:span`
  holding its text, inside one `description`, the shape Zotero uses for a
  link inside a sentence. The two then share a line box and a baseline.
  `provider-rows` writes the reason into the span, so a repaint no longer
  touches the link.
- **Verified live on beta7**, by Range rects over the text nodes: credits
  and Add more time, and the reason and Log in, both tiers, `top` and
  `bottom` deltas 0, gaps 6.00 px. The run used the kit's `00` and `99`

## The global speed became a setting: the copy must come first, and one writer spreads its own (2026-09-29 14:38, issue #82)

- **Where the speed lived.** The global speed was the `speed` field of
  `readAloud.memory`, an undeclared JSON pref kept outside `DEFAULTS`
  since #41, so neither the backup nor the sync of #68 carried it. It is
  now `readAloud.speedPercent`, an int pref in hundredths (ADR 0012); the
  JSON keeps only the legacy voice. Gecko drops a user value equal to the
  default, so a profile at 1.0× holds no user value at all.
- **The one-time copy runs first.** `migrateGlobalSpeed` is the startup
  step after the strings. Later would be wrong twice: the OpenAI split
  (#113) calls `writeMemory`, which now writes the JSON without its old
  speed, and the settings sync's per-key observers would stamp the copy as
  a change made now, so the last computer to update would push its speed
  to all. Unstamped, it goes nowhere: `mergeSharedSettings` pushes a key
  only with a stamp (`myTs > 0`), and a seeded profile never seeds again.
- **"The volume applies at once" stopped being true with #121.** #68's
  plan applied every non-provider setting at once while a tab read. Since
  #121, `PLAYBACK_SETTINGS` (`settings-impact.ts`) makes the reading
  impact report every open player, paused included, for the volume, the
  pauses, prefetch and the two switches, so the sync defers them and a
  restore that changes them is refused. The global speed joined that list.
  Found only after the owner had been told the volume applies at once, and
  the question was put again.
- **Two writers of one pref.** memory-sync now observes the global speed
  and spreads a value it did not write, which is how a synced or restored
  speed reaches idle readers and Zotero's per-language entries. The pane's
  slider writes the pref too, through `setDefaultSpeed`, which writes the
  pref first and the managers after. In a unit test whose playing manager
  was not among the readers the observer walks, the observer's spread
  wrote the new speed into Zotero's entries first; the manager then
  persisted its fallback voice with a speed the entry already held, and
  `noteVoicesChange` took the voice moving alone for a pick. In Zotero
  both walk the same readers, but correctness should not hang on it. So
  `setDefaultSpeed` marks its own run (`settingDefaultSpeed()`), and the
  observer leaves such a write to it. memory-sync's own writes are
  recognized by the last speed it wrote.

## zotero.org writes time left as credits over price, rounded up, and Zotero's two out-of-time errors are distinct words (2026-09-29 23:10, issue #140)

- **zotero.org/settings/readaloud shows minutes, not credits**, and they
  are Zotero's own sum: the owner's page read "Standard Voice Minutes
  Remaining: 1 hour 54 minutes" and "Premium Voice Minutes Remaining: 9
  minutes to 26 minutes, depending on voice" for the 114 and 260 credits
  the plugin read that morning. 114 ÷ 1 = 114; 260 ÷ 30 = 8.67 → 9 and
  260 ÷ 10 = 26: credits over each price, rounded up, the range running
  from the dearest price to the cheapest. A range answers the objection
  that made #159 show credits alone, and the settings now show the time.
- **Zotero's `formatTimeRemaining`** (reader.js 38417-38440) is
  `Math.ceil`, then days, hours and minutes through
  `Intl.DurationFormat(undefined, { style: 'narrow', daysDisplay: 'auto',
  hoursDisplay: 'auto', minutesDisplay: 'always' })`, with a
  `1d 2h 3m` fallback where the platform lacks it. Zotero 10 runs on
  Firefox 140 (`platform.ini` Milestone 140.15.0), which has it; Node 22
  does not, so unit tests see the fallback. In macOS JavaScriptCore the
  narrow form is `1h 54m` in en-US and `1小时54分钟` in zh-CN, so Chinese
  has no shorter form; not yet measured in Zotero itself.
- **Two words, not one, for running out** (`syncAPIClient.js` 717-721):
  a 402 whose body is `daily_limit_exceeded` becomes
  `daily-limit-exceeded`, any other 402 `quota-exceeded`. The Engine
  passes both through (`core/engine/session.ts`); the Player read only
  `quota-exceeded` and called the daily limit a connection failure. A
  plugin provider's rate limit is also `quota-exceeded`
  (`core/providers/errors.ts`), so the used-up alert is only for Zotero's
  Standard or Premium selected.
- **What Zotero's own player did with them**: red under
  `URGENT_THRESHOLD_MINUTES = 3` (82160), or on `quota-exceeded`
  (82254-82256); the used-up message offered "Add more time or continue
  reading with Standard Voices" (`reader.ftl` 230-233), which the Player
  does not copy — a switch would spend another tier's credits. Its
  voice list sorted by price with a divider between prices
  (`buildVoiceOptions`, 38442-38481), and its log-in row replaced the
  tier dropdown only while signed out with one tier on offer
  (38855-38874); neither was carried over.
- **Each voice's time is Zotero's own getter**: a Zotero voice's
  `minutesRemaining` reads its provider's `standardCreditsRemaining` or
  `premiumCreditsRemaining` over its `creditsPerMinute`
  (39256-39263). The provider's figures come from the `tts/voices`
  headers and the manager's 60 s `tts/credits` poll, which the Engine's
  controller keeps for a Zotero voice (`engine/controller.ts` 128-138),
  so the Player's time, read from the snapshot every 250 ms, moves once
  a minute while reading. After `quota-exceeded` the credits can still
  read positive until that poll, so the Player shows the selected voice
  as 0m from the error itself.
