## The WebDAV timeout ended at the headers, and one stalled reply wedged its sync for the session (2026-10-04 02:15, issue #169)

Found while reviewing #168, an automated pull request that capped the
size of a WebDAV reply and claimed the 15 s timeout already handled slow
connections. It did not:

- **`withTimeout` around `fetch()` ends when the headers arrive.**
  `request()` in `src/core/webdav.ts` wrapped `deps.fetch(...)` only;
  `download()` and `list()` then awaited `response.text()` outside any
  bound. Measured in Node with the real `Response`: `timeoutMs: 50`, a
  200 whose body never came, still pending after 500 ms.
- **A run that never settles wedges `single-flight.ts`.** While
  `inFlight` is set, every `poke` and `flush` joins one trailing run,
  started only in the in-flight run's `finally`. All three WebDAV
  transports (settings sync, position sync, the Positions File) await
  `client.download(...)` inside their run, so one stalled reply stopped
  that sync for the rest of the session, with no error and no log line.
  The pull before a resume is bounded on its own (`RESUME_PULL_MS`,
  2 s), so reading still started after the wait; the shutdown flush too
  (`SYNC_SHUTDOWN_TIMEOUT_MS`). The settings window's WebDAV buttons
  share one `busy` flag, cleared only when the action settles.
- Not measured: whether Gecko's own network handling eventually breaks
  a connection whose server stops sending mid-body.

The body is now read by `readText` (`src/core/webdav.ts`) through its
stream reader: every chunk within the timeout (the gap is bounded, not
the whole body, so a slow but steady reply finishes), at most 10 MiB
(`WEBDAV_MAX_REPLY_BYTES`, #168's cap; a declared `Content-Length` above
it is refused unread), the stream cancelled on any failure, and a stream
that breaks reported as `WebDAVError('network', …)`. A body locked by
`response.text()` cannot be cancelled, and the sandbox has no
AbortController, so `withTimeout(response.text())` alone would have
left the connection open behind the rejection. Whether
`response.body.getReader()` works in the plugin sandbox is what the live
case `test/zotero-dev/cases/webdav-reply-bounds.md` proves first.

## Cancelling a fetch body's stream does not close Gecko's connection; aborting the request's signal does (2026-10-04 11:16, issue #169)

The live run of `test/zotero-dev/cases/webdav-reply-bounds.md` on
1.16.5-beta (zotero-tester, Zotero 10.0.6-beta.1, a local stub server)
proved the stream path in the plugin sandbox and found the entry above
wrong on one point:

- **`reader.cancel()` / `body.cancel()` leaves the socket open.** After
  a stalled download and a stalled listing had failed at 15.1 s and
  15.0 s with the right error, their sockets to the stub were still
  ESTABLISHED 10 minutes later, also after `Cu.forceGC()` and
  `Cu.forceCC()`, and the stub saw no close. Only the reply still
  streaming (64 MiB of spaces) was closed, at 43 ms, by the server's own
  write failing (EPIPE), 22,544,384 bytes in. An `AbortController`
  abort from chrome scope closed its socket at once (`client-fin`), as
  the catalog's listings had measured on 2026-09-06.
- **Gecko's `fetch` resolves only once a body byte arrives.** A 200 with
  `Content-Length: 10485761` and nothing after the head left the fetch
  pending (still pending at 4 s from chrome scope) and the client
  failed at the headers timeout, `No reply … within 15 s`, at 15.1 s;
  with one body byte after the head the sandbox refused it in 44 ms as
  larger than 10 MB. A head + first fragment (`/stall/`) resolved in
  7 ms.
- **Node's `res.writeHead()` alone sends nothing**: the headers leave on
  the first body write, or with `res.flushHeaders()`. The stub's first
  `/declared/` never sent its head at all.
- A broken reply reads as `broke off: Error in input stream` (16 ms).

So since 1.16.5-beta2 `createWebDAVClient` takes `newAbortController`
(`WebDAVDeps`), makes one controller per request, hands its `signal` to
`fetch` and aborts it on every failure: the headers timeout (which
leaked the same way before), a stall, the cap, a broken stream; the
stream is still cancelled afterwards, the only release when no
controller could be made. The plugin's clients take
`newChromeAbortController` (`webdavDeps()` in `src/index.ts`), the
settings window's the pane's `newPaneAbortController`. Why it matters
beyond a socket: Gecko keeps a handful of connections per server, so a
server that stalled again and again could fill them, and every later
request would queue behind the dead ones.

`withTimeout` (`src/core/timeout.ts`) now rejects before it calls
`onTimeout`: an abort that rejects the request at once (in the unit
test, a fetch rejecting synchronously on its signal's `abort` event)
otherwise won the race, and the headers timeout read `Cannot reach …:
aborted` instead of `No reply … within 15 s`. `system/mac.ts` already
guarded against the same race for its kill.
