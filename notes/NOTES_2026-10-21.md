## A click outside the Player reaches its page as a blur, or not at all (2026-10-21 19:20, issue #213)

The Player's page is a document of its own, an iframe in the reader's
page, and the PDF, EPUB or snapshot view is another iframe beside it. Its
menus closed only on a pointerdown or an Escape in its own document, so a
click anywhere else left the menu open and the frame at the 460 px it
grows to for a menu, a transparent box over the page that takes the wheel
and the clicks.

What the Player's page can hear of a click outside it, read in the
installed 11.0 bundle (`resource/reader/reader.js`):

- **A click in the view** moves the focus into the view's iframe: the
  Player's focused element gets a focusout and its window a `blur`, and
  nothing else (the PDF view's pointerdown does not cancel, ~81134-81160;
  the DOM view's only for the note tool, ~54961-54976).
- **A click on the reader's own toolbar or sidebar** does not move the
  focus at all. `FocusManager._handlePointerDown` (83964-83986) cancels
  pointerdown (83979-83981) on everything in the reader's page except inputs,
  annotations, the thumbnails, the Outline, the error bar, reference rows,
  the preview and Appearance popups and `#selector`, so that a toolbar
  click leaves the focus in the view. The Player keeps it there too, and
  hears no blur.
- **Nothing moves the focus by itself while reading.** `ReadAloudManager`
  calls no `focus()`; Zotero moves the focus on its own only in
  `openContextMenu` (87898) and when the Appearance popup closes (88087),
  both after a click outside the Player.

So the menu closes on two signals: the page's `window` blur, and a
capture-phase pointerdown on the reader's window, which the host turns
into a call of the page's exported `zttsClosePopover` (`closeMenu` in
`src/index.ts`, `Reflect.apply` on the waived window like `renderLayout`).
Events inside the Player's frame never reach the reader's window, so its
own clicks are untouched. Whether Gecko really keeps the focus in the
Player on a cancelled toolbar pointerdown is read from the source, not
seen; the host's pointerdown covers that case whichever way it goes.

## The Skip keys took the arrows from the Outline and the sliders (2026-10-21 19:20, issue #212)

Zotero's `FocusManager._handleKeyDown` (84013-84016; first written here as
the KeyboardManager's, corrected the same day) returns before its own arrow
handling when the target is inside `.outline-view` or is an
`input[type="range"]` and the key is ArrowLeft or ArrowRight, with any
modifiers; the Outline's handler (35015-35022) expands and collapses on
Shift+Arrow as well. The shortcuts' capturing listeners consumed both Skip
pairs there while a session was open. They now leave exactly those keys
to exactly those widgets, checked against the event's target and against
the deep focused element, since the chrome window's listener, the primary
one (NOTES 2026-08-22), may hear a reader's key with the reader's frame as
its target.

Left as it is, and put to the owner rather than widened here:

- **Zotero steps between the items of a focus group with all four
  arrows** (`tabToItem`, 84017-84022: toolbar buttons, sidebar
  annotations, thumbnails), and the Skip and volume keys still take those
  while a session is open.
- **The library's trees**, read in the code and not run: `pickReader`
  answers a speaking reader whatever tab is selected, so while a
  background tab speaks, Left/Right in the item or collection tree Skip
  that reading instead of collapsing and expanding rows.

## The speed keys stepped from a speed nobody reads (2026-10-21 19:20, issue #197)

A tab's manager learns its language only when its Player opens
(`_prepareReadAloud`), and until then `adjust()` stepped from Zotero's
`reader.readAloudVoices`. A profile with no entry there sent every press
to the Global speed (`rememberSpeed`), which that read never looked at:
since #82 the Global speed is `readAloud.speedPercent`, not an entry. Each
press therefore started from 1.0 again, and a Global speed set from the
voice browser was overwritten with 1.05.

An idle tab now steps from the speed it will read at: the Global speed
while *Use one speed everywhere* is on; with it off, Zotero's stored speed,
and the Global speed only when no entry holds one, since that is where the
press is kept. The Global speed follows every press synchronously either
way: through `rememberSpeed` with no entry, and through memory-sync's
observer of Zotero's pref with one, which learns the speed inside the
write (`src/read-aloud/memory-sync.ts:297-313`). `readPersistedSpeed`
keeps its meaning, what Zotero stored or 1, for its other callers; the
null-returning `findPersistedSpeed` is the new half.

## Verified live on 1.16.13-beta2, and Zotero 11 skips on the arrows itself (2026-10-21 22:15, issues #213, #212, #197)

`zotero-tester` ran the cases on Zotero 11.0-beta.1 with the build of
`8bf3240` (bundle sha256 `5119a6b6…`), after a first run was cut off by a
30-minute limit before any check.

- **#213 passed in all three layouts**, the voice list and the speed menu
  against a click on the PDF page, the EPUB page, the toolbar's zoom
  button, the sidebar's Outline and inside the Appearance popup (10 of 10
  each): the menu gone, the frame back to 34 px in the bars and to the
  panel's own height in floating, the click doing its own work. On the
  toolbar click the Player's page kept the focus (`document.hasFocus()`
  true before and after), which settles what was read from the source
  above: only the host's pointerdown closes the menu there. A voice list
  left open while reading stayed open over three sentences. The floating
  panel is 222 px expanded (128 collapsed) while the remaining-time row
  shows, 20 px over the 202 / 108 without it.
- **#197 passed**: on a fixture tab whose Player was never opened (`lang`
  null) Shift+C ×3 took `speedPercent` 145 → 150 → 155 → 160 with the
  toasts `1.5×`, `1.55×`, `1.6×` (the toast drops a trailing zero), Shift+X
  back to 145, and the Player opened afterwards read 1.45×. In this
  profile every entry of `reader.readAloudVoices` holds the Global speed,
  so the run cannot tell the new starting point from the old; the profile
  with no entry, where the keys stuck at 1.05×, stays the unit tests'.
- **#212 failed live, and not by the plugin.** With the Outline focused
  (`Services.focus.focusedElement` = `DIV#outlineView`) and a paused
  session, a trusted ArrowRight still skipped a sentence. A research probe
  wrapped the manager's `skipAhead` / `skipBack` to record their caller:
  the one call came from `reader.js:84550`, Zotero's own
  `KeyboardManager._handleKeyDown`, whose Read Aloud block (84523-84552)
  takes ArrowLeft/ArrowRight (sentence) and Alt+Arrow (paragraph) whenever
  `readAloudActive` and the target is not a button or a select, with no
  exception for the Outline. The same probe with the focus elsewhere
  recorded the plugin's own skip (`zotero-tts.js`), so it tells the two
  apart. The plugin's listeners let the key pass: one listener of the
  current instance on the main window and one on each reader window
  (`nsIEventListenerService`), no leftover of an older instance. The same
  Read Aloud block, with its Space, H and U, is recorded for 10.0.x
  (NOTES 2026-08-26, 2026-09-26); its arrows there were not read.
- **No slider to test**: the Appearance popup of this build shows no
  `input[type="range"]` for either fixture (Reading Mode, Scrolling,
  Spreads, Split View and Themes only).
- **Driving notes**: Firefox 153 has no `nsIDOMWindowUtils.sendMouseEvent`;
  trusted clicks go through `sendNativeMouseEvent` and need the Zotero
  window active, which the harness takes away between bridge calls. A
  debug keydown listener the tester left on the main window was found by
  the listener probe and removed. Every pref the runs touched was found
  as before, `reader.readAloudVoices` byte for byte against the run's
  preflight snapshot.
